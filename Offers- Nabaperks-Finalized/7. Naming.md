**"The 28-Day First-Regular Launch"**

- **Measurable goal:** bringing a first regular back.
- **Container word:** Launch, reflecting the done-for-you delivery.
- **Audience line:** Always pair it with “for single-site UK food-led pubs” in the surrounding headline or description.

**Campaign wrapper retained for separately approved use:** "The 28-Day Gastropub Mid-Week Revenue Accelerator". It is not the default public name.

### The Nabaperks Naming Shortlist

Because offers naturally fatigue over time in local markets (once everyone in your target radius has seen it), Hormozi advises refreshing the name rather than changing the product. Here is your ready-to-deploy shortlist of alternate "wrappers" depending on the specific objection you are trying to overcome:

1.  **The 28-Day First-Regular Launch (DFY):** This is the primary public wrapper. It avoids an implied revenue outcome and aligns with the measurable First-Regular Guarantee.
2.  **The Done-For-You Pub Card Launch:** Use this when the prospect's main objection is "software setup anxiety." It leads directly with your delivery model.
3.  **The No-App Midweek Regulars Launch:** Use this when you are competing against app-based loyalty vendors. It highlights the technical advantage (no app required) alongside the midweek goal.
4.  **The Counter-Ready Loyalty Launch:** Good for owners who fear "another login nobody uses." It emphasises the physical presence of the posters and the QR code live at the till.

### The Naming Rules & Claims Boundary (Crucial for Protection)

To maintain Lapen Inns' integrity and avoid legal risks, your sales and marketing teams must adhere to these strict naming rules:

- **The Product Identity:** Always keep **"Nabaperks"** as the permanent company/product noun.
- **The Billing Identity:** Always keep **"Growth Plan"** as the official Stripe/plan noun.
- **The Offer Wrapper:** Use words like **"Accelerator"** or **"Launch"** only for the offer wrappers.
- **The Revenue Rule:** "Revenue Accelerator" is a name, **not a contractual promise** of revenue. On paid ads and public web pages, you must prefer names that do not imply guaranteed revenue (like "The 28-Day First-Regular Launch") unless legal and compliance have approved the exact line.

### What to do when the name fatigues

When your ads eventually stop converting, Hormozi's framework dictates a specific order of operations to refresh the offer without creating operational drag. You change the imagery first, then the copy, and then the **Headline/Wrapper**. You only change the actual duration, price, or core mechanics as an absolute last resort.
