### 1. Honest Scarcity: The Growth Rate Cap

You are going to cap the number of pubs you bring on each week. This creates a highly desirable "waitlist" dynamic where prospects must act to secure their spot.

- **The Policy:** Lapen Inns will onboard a **maximum of 5 new DFY pubs per week**.
- **The Sales Script/Headline:** _"We only onboard 5 new pubs a week to ensure we can fully manage your launch and physically print your materials. We have [N] spots left this week."_
- **The Execution Rule:** You must update the "[N] spots left" from a real weekly capacity tracker before every sales conversation. If the week is full, tell them they must wait until the next week—this proves your scarcity is real and increases their desire.

### 2. Urgency: Rolling Launch Urgency

Since you are selling an ongoing service, you will use "Rolling Urgency" to force a decision based on real-world operational cutoffs.

- **The Strategy:** Tie the prospect's decision to a real calendar outcome, such as your physical poster print-batch cutoff, or a "go-live" date right before a notoriously quiet trading week they care about.
- **The Sales Script/Headline:** _"If you confirm this week, we can complete your setup and get your first poster run into the next print batch, ensuring you are live before [agreed date/upcoming quiet week]."_

### 3. Urgency: Seasonal Promo Alignment

You can artificially stimulate demand by rotating the "wrapper" of your offer based on the season (e.g., "Spring Pub Revenue Promo").

- **The Strategy:** When a product promo is live (e.g., a time-limited free print-and-post window enabled in the software's marketing configuration), you use the promo's end date to drive immediate action.
- **The Execution Rule:** If the promotion already includes free poster printing, do not double-charge them. Position your £99 fee strictly as your human configuration labor, with the printing included "free" under the promo.

### 🚫 The "Do Not Do" List (Claims Boundary)

To maintain your premium positioning and protect your brand's integrity, you must ruthlessly avoid pressure tactics that cheapen the brand.

**You are strictly forbidden from using:**

- **Fake countdowns:** Resetting countdown timers on your website.
- **Fake software scarcity:** Claiming you are "nearly sold out" of the digital Growth Plan alone.
- **Fake pricing urgency:** Saying "the price rises tonight!" without a documented, real price change.
- **High-pressure closes:** Demanding a "same-call close" for a £99 setup.

By deploying this stack, you are telling the gastropub owner: _"We want to help you, but our human capacity is genuinely limited to 5 pubs a week, and we need your answer before our print-batch cutoff."_ It is honest, highly ethical, and will make your ideal prospects sprint to hand you their £99 setup fee.
