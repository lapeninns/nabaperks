## 2. Pressure tests against the data

### Would 56 days have saved the rewards that expired?

No reward has expired. The 30-day clock started on 5 September, so the first answer arrives on 5 October. What the data does say: everyone who has collected did so within 8 days of the ready date; 23 rewards have been open more than 7 days and **every one belongs to a member who has not visited since completing the card** (Q019). Extending 30 → 56 days only helps members who come back between day 30 and day 56, and no member in this dataset has yet returned after a gap longer than 11 days. It is cheap, harmless, and unlikely to move the number. The expiry problem you suspect is a **lapsed-member** problem; the lever is re-engagement (which needs a channel, #14), not the calendar.

### Which venues have enough midweek activity for windows to be meaningful?

There are no opening hours in the system, so I used each venue's own stamp and redemption timing (Q020, Q025–Q028):

Reading: a **quiet-hours** window (Mon–Thu lunch) is nearly empty at every venue: under one member a day except at Bell. A **quiet-days** window (Mon–Thu, normal evening hours) covers 30–70% of visits and, because of the current weekday rule, already 50–70% of redemptions. Railway and Old School House do not fit "Mon–Thu is quiet" at all (Sunday is Railway's busiest day; Saturday is Old School House's quietest). Only Bell and Barley Mow have enough volume to configure windows from evidence; the rest would be configuring from the owner's intuition, which the data partly contradicts. Recommendation: windows as **days**, per venue, defaulting to each venue's two quietest weekdays from this table, revisited monthly.

### Where the data contradicts the proposal

1. **Longer waits lose redemptions, sharply.** Rewards completed Mon–Thu (1-day hold) are 70% redeemed; completed Friday (3-day hold to Monday) **10%**; Sat–Sun 50% (Q023). Half of all redemptions happen on the first ready day. A windows model that pushes a Friday completion to "the next quiet window" (Monday or later) reproduces the worst-performing case for the 40% of cards that complete Fri–Sun. If windows are a gate rather than a bonus, expect the overall redemption rate to fall from ~50% toward the Friday figure for weekend completers.
2. **Collection is already a spending visit.** 73% of collections were followed by a stamp within 3 hours (Q025). Forcing collection into a separate quiet window converts one visit into two, and the second one is the one people skip. The current "next day, any time" rule is what makes the reward visit coincide with the next purchase.
3. **No lock is expensive on a 3-stamp card.** 77% of redemptions come from near-daily visitors. With a join stamp, a collection-day stamp and 3 stamps per card, a daily regular today cycles roughly every 3 days of visits plus a median 1.9-day lock plus the hold. Removing the lock and the hold shortens that to about 3 days: ≈40–60% more rewards for the regulars segment, ≈+35–50% overall (Q026 model: ≈55 extra completed cards in 15 days across 9 venues at the upper bound; lower bound from observed activity-while-locked is single digits). Either raise `stamps_required` (DB accepts up to 99; the app caps at 6) or accept the cost consciously. The data says the lock is currently _short_ for the people it affects (median 1.9 days), so the gain from removing it is mostly for regulars, i.e. the people already coming.
4. **Windows are undiscoverable without a channel.** 6 push subscriptions. Today members learn a reward is ready by opening the card; under windows they would learn "come back Tuesday" at the bar on a Saturday night. Build #14 before #6, or windows will read as refusals.
5. **93% of rewards are alcohol and every collection requires an 18+ ID check** (69 of 92 open rewards are held by members with no or unverified DOB). Quiet-window collection could actually help owners run ID checks calmly, but it also means the windows must be staffed by the owner, since only the owner can collect. Worth noting when choosing windows.
6. **"Monthly diners" do not exist yet.** The population is 53% one-time and 44% weekly-or-better; the fortnightly segment is 3% and monthly is 0%. Any policy designed around infrequent-but-returning members is designing for a segment this data cannot see. Revisit after 60 days.
7. **Minimum spend and one-transaction rules have no enforcement surface.** There is no staff or POS in the loop; the only actor is the owner at collection. Building these means re-introducing a staff/receipt concept that was deliberately excised.

### Where the data supports the proposal

- **Atomic issue + no lock + wallet** are structurally cheap and remove NBS02, the heal sweep, the `card_full` referral hold (one held referral has generated 584 retry events) and the admin-cancel stranding.
- **Trading-day boundary**: the venue code already rolls at 05:00; aligning the stamp day removes a real inconsistency (a member stamped at 23:50 cannot stamp at 00:10).
- **Policy version + snapshot**: no versioning exists and four copies of the reward picker plus three TS readiness mirrors already exist; a single versioned predicate is overdue.
- **Service vs marketing separation**: the category model is there; only the classification and the channel are wrong.
- **Availability at request time, rechecked at scan**: already exactly this.

## 3. Recommended build order

1. **Foundations and bug fixes (S, no behaviour change).** Add `policy_version`/`policy_snapshot` to `reward_events` and `loyalty_cards`, backfill `legacy_v1`; add the unique `(membership_id, cycle_number)` index; make `admin_cancel_reward` release the cycle; add the 1-redemption-per-day check; reclassify birthday/direct rewards as transactional; fix the referral push payload; add a category-map parity test.
2. **Single readiness predicate (M).** Introduce `private.reward_collection_state()` in SQL and make the eligibility function, the transition, `get_reward_scan_context` and one TS helper call it; delete the other TS mirrors. Branch on `policy_version`. This is the seam every later change goes through.
3. **Venue trading day (S).** Per-venue boundary defaulting to 05:00; use it for both stamps and venue code.
4. **No-lock cycle open (M).** Behind `policy_version = v2` for new cards; one-off migration opens the next cycle for the 92 locked memberships. Pair it with a decision on `stamps_required` (recommend piloting 4 or 5 at one venue) because of contradiction #3.
5. **Service channel (M).** SMS/WhatsApp transactional via the existing Twilio integration for "reward ready", "window opens", "expires soon". Without this, step 6 is not viable.
6. **Collection days, then windows (L).** Pilot whole quiet _days_ at Bell and Barley Mow (the only venues with enough data), collectable at any hour, and keep next-day-any-time collection for weekend completions. Measure redemption by issue weekday for a month before narrowing to hours.
7. **Windows-based expiry and closures (M).** Only after 6 proves out; the 56-day figure can ship earlier as a plain setting change since the schema already allows 1–3660 days.
8. **Suspension grace (S/M).** Low priority: no billed venues today.
9. **Minimum spend / one-transaction (L).** Defer until there is a purchase signal; the data offers nothing to justify the operational cost now.

## 4. Open questions for you

1. Does "reward-only collection earns no stamp" mean "no purchase, no stamp" (needs a purchase signal) or "a collection visit never earns" (removes the stamp 73% of members get on collection day)?
2. Are windows meant as a **gate** (collect only then) or a **bonus** (collect any trading day, plus something extra in quiet windows)? The Friday data argues strongly for the latter.
3. Is raising `stamps_required` on the table alongside removing the lock? Without it, reward cost rises for the regulars segment.
4. Which channel is acceptable for service messages: SMS, WhatsApp, email, or push only? 412 members ticked an offers box that was recorded as SMS consent; that is marketing consent, not a service-message basis, so the legal position of a transactional SMS needs confirming.
5. Should the 18+ / photo-ID requirement stay on every reward, or become an attribute of the pool item (alcohol only) as part of the policy snapshot?
6. Do you want the pack in `queries.sql` re-run on 6 October and 4 November to answer the expiry and rhythm questions with real data before step 6 is designed?
